

PrintGrid is a digital print-layout planning and optimization application that 
calculates design placement and paper utilization, and estimated waste based on defined canvas and dimensions

PrintGrid calculates and displays the percentage of paper 
used and the percentage of paper remaining based on the current layout.

PrintGrid calculates the number of stickers that can fit within a selected 
canvas based on the dimensions of the stickers and available canvas space.

PrintGrid stores completed projects, allowing users to access review
and download previously created projects.

PrintGrid supports different measurement units, which can be toggled through the settings.

PrintGrid supports multiple objects or designs within a single canvas, 
allowing different designs to be arranged and calculated together.

PrintGrid provides 28 different paper sized canvases for a project.

PrintGrid supports multiple image file formats, 
including PNG, JPG, WEBP, TIFF, GIF, and BMP, with a maximum file size of 25 MB per file.

PrintGrid takes an average of 2 seconds to lay stickers ,it takes an average of 5 minutes to lay them manually

PrintGrid analyzes the arrangement of designs on the selected canvas to provide estimated sticker quantity, paper utilization, and paper waste calculations.







PrintGrid is a print-production planning and layout optimization application that helps printers determine 
how designs should be arranged on a sheet, how many pieces can be produced,
 how much material will be used, and how much may be wasted before printing.


our planner can consider:

multiple designs
different quantities
sheet dimensions
custom sheet sizes
margins
horizontal spacing
vertical spacing
rotation
grouping designs
sheet cost
orientation



PrintGrid removes the manual calculations and repetitive preparation around printing.

A printer shouldn't have to manually experiment with:

A4 → 12 pieces → doesn't fit → rotate → 15 pieces → change spacing → try again → calculate sheets → calculate waste...

PrintGrid should make that computational problem almost invisible.
